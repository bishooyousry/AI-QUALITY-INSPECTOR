# Facilitator guide — AI QUALITY INSPECTOR: THE EVIDENCE LAB (online)

**Goal:** participants can test an AI output for accuracy, fairness and completeness before trusting it — and decide what to change before it is used at work.
**Format:** individual, 8–10 minutes of play + 5–7 minutes of debrief. (In the classroom this is a 6-minute pairs activity; you can let neighbours compare findings while they play.)

## Know the source before you present
- The sample output, the three questions, the debrief questions and the facilitator tip below come from the course notes. The worksheet handout is not in the deck.
- The extra records (sales summary, campaign timeline, history, customer survey) are **simulated, fictional training evidence** written for this game. They are consistent with one another and are labelled on screen.

## Before the session (5 min)
- Open the link → Trainer (PIN 2026). One session per group (**+ New session**). Put the **player link** (or the 5-character code) in the chat.
- Read the sample output aloud: “Sales increased by exactly 47% last month thanks to our new marketing strategy, making it the best month in company history. Every customer who was contacted responded positively.”

## Run (timing)
| Time | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Say: “Here is an AI-written output. Before anyone trusts it, test it against three questions — is it accurate, is it fair, is it complete — and decide what you would change.” Share link/code. |
| 0:01 | Everyone is in. Optionally open **Projector view**. |
| 0:01–0:10 | Watch the table: claim statuses (U unsupported · X contradicted · V verified · N not assessable), the three gate verdicts, rewrite checks (x/6), release decision. Expand a row (⌄) to read the sanitized rewrite and notes. |
| ~0:10 | Ask 2 volunteers to share one thing they found. Then **Reveal leaderboard**. |
| 0:10–0:17 | Debrief (below). |

## What happens (for narration)
1. **Incoming report** — the draft arrives; you are the quality inspector.
2. **Evidence scan** — four claims; open the simulated records, link them, judge each claim, ask for what is missing.
3. **Three gates** — Accuracy, Fairness, Completeness; “insufficient evidence” is allowed but must be justified.
4. **Repair** — rewrite the statement; live transparent checks show which safeguards are present.
5. **Release decision** — approve, revise or hold: the best choice depends on the evidence and on the participant’s own rewrite.
6. **Quality report** — copy or download it.

## What the evidence shows (answer key for you — also available inside the dashboard as **ANSWER KEY (trainer only)**, in English and Arabic)
- **47%** — *Unsupported*: the comparison base is not stated; West is missing; it reproduces only as +47.0% year-on-year for the three reported regions (+12.8% vs last month); “exactly” is false precision.
- **“thanks to our new marketing strategy”** — *Unsupported*: a seasonal promotion (1 Sep) and a 10% discount (8 Sep) pre-date the campaign (18 Sep); sales were already rising; no control group.
- **“the best month in company history”** — *Contradicted*: December 2024 = 7.30; September cannot exceed 4.85 + 1.40 = 6.25 even if West hit its record. Missing pre-2022 records cannot rescue it.
- **“Every customer … responded positively”** — *Contradicted*: 180 of 1,200 responded; 36 neutral, 18 negative; 1,020 said nothing.
- **Fairness** — concerns, not proof: the West region is absent from the data and the survey, only 15% answered, one initiative is credited. “No bias found” is not “fair”; harm cannot be proven from this data.
- **Completeness** — incomplete: no comparison base, West missing, no response rate, other factors ignored, nothing said about what still needs verifying.

## Debrief questions
1. *(Course notes)* Did you find anything wrong or missing in this output?
2. *(Course notes)* How long did the check take — was it worth the time?
3. *(Course tip)* If someone says “it was perfect”: what would you check if this went straight to a client or your manager? The exact 47% and “every customer responded positively” are the two red flags most groups should catch.
4. *(Suggested)* What is the difference between “unsupported” and “contradicted”? Which of your claims were which, and what evidence decided it?
5. *(Suggested)* Why is “no bias found” not the same as “fair”? What would you need to see to judge fairness?
6. *(Suggested)* Which release decision did you take — approve, revise or hold — and what in the evidence or in your rewrite decided it? Who should verify the missing data?

## Notes
- The rewrite checks are keyword/structure rules, not a quality verdict. Read two or three sanitized rewrites aloud and let the group judge the wording.
- Scoring rewards evidence-based judgment and honest uncertainty, not speed. Use it for feedback, not for grading people.
- If the live connection drops: **Refresh** on the dashboard rebuilds from the relay history; otherwise collect the downloaded report files and use **Import**.
- Export CSV at the end if you want the sanitized results.
